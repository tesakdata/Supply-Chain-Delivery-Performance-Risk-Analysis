# Supply Chain Delivery Performance & Risk Analysis

This project analyzes delivery performance, disruption risk, supplier reliability, and logistics efficiency across a multi-category supply chain portfolio. The goal is to identify key operational inefficiencies, quantify their financial impact, and provide data-driven recommendations to improve on-time delivery and reduce risk exposure.

The analysis was conducted using a structured dataset of 1,000 orders, covering product categories, suppliers, shipping modes, delays, disruptions, and operational metrics.

---

## Project Objectives

- Evaluate overall delivery performance and reliability
- Identify high-risk suppliers, categories, and shipping modes
- Quantify the financial impact of delays and disruptions
- Assess supplier reliability and concentration risk
- Deliver practical recommendations to improve supply chain resilience

---

## Key Insights

| Area                    | Finding |
|-------------------------|--------|
| **On-Time Delivery**    | Only **51%** of orders delivered on time |
| **Average Delay**       | **1.94 days** per order (systemic issue) |
| **High-Risk Categories**| Electronics ($5.69M) and Textiles ($5.23M) — 43% of portfolio value with poorest performance |
| **Shipping Modes**      | Air: 48% delayed<br>Rail & Sea: ~54% delayed |
| **Disruption Rate**     | **51.4%** of orders flagged as at-risk |
| **Supplier Reliability**| Average 74.6% (weak correlation with delays) |
| **Financial Impact**    | **$777,000** estimated annual cost due to delays |

**Key Observations:**
- Highest-value categories have the worst delivery performance
- Top 10 suppliers control **41.7%** of total spend (high concentration risk)
- Delays are mostly process-driven (shortages & customs) rather than seasonal

---

## Recommendations

### Immediate Actions (0–30 Days)
- Resolve internal process bottlenecks causing systemic delays
- Optimize inventory to reduce stock shortages
- Reassign high-value shipments to better-performing transport modes

### Short-Term Actions (30–90 Days)
- Implement customs pre-clearance procedures
- Reduce dependency on Sea and Rail for time-sensitive goods
- Begin supplier diversification for critical categories

### Long-Term Actions (90–180 Days)
- Deploy formal **supplier performance scorecards**
- Introduce reliability-based procurement gating
- Build a Financial Delay Impact Dashboard
- Establish disruption escalation protocols

---

## KPIs Tracked

- On-Time Delivery Rate
- Average Delay Days
- Supplier Reliability Score
- Disruption Frequency & Severity
- Shipping Mode Performance
- Lead Time (Order to Delivery)
- Financial Delay Impact



---

## Key Takeaway

This project reveals a critical **misalignment between value, performance, and risk** in the supply chain.

  - High-value categories are also the worst-performing
  - Delivery delays are systemic rather than seasonal
  - Supplier reliability has a moderate but non-linear relationship with delays
  - Logistics and disruption factors play a stronger role than supplier scoring alone

By improving supplier management, optimizing logistics allocation, and addressing process bottlenecks can increase on-time delivery beyond 70% and significantly reduce operational cost leakage.
